Dialogs interrupt for a single focused decision — a confirm, a destructive action, a short
form. They sit on a dimmed overlay with the highest [elevation](/foundations/elevation). Open
it:

<DialogShowcase />

## Anatomy

- **Overlay** — dims the page and closes the dialog on click-outside.
- **Panel** — a `popover`-token surface with `lg` radius and the `high` shadow.
- **Title + body** — one clear question, one short line of context.
- **Actions** — right-aligned; the primary or destructive action last.

## Button layouts

The footer carries one or two buttons, never more. The primary or destructive action always
sits last (rightmost on desktop, bottom on mobile).

<DialogButtonsShowcase />

- **Single primary** — an acknowledgement with nothing to decide ("You're all set" → *Got it*).
  No Cancel: there's no alternative to offer.
- **Primary + secondary** — a normal confirm. The secondary (Cancel) is the safe way out; the
  primary commits the action ("Pair this device?" → *Cancel* / *Pair*).
- **Destructive + secondary** — same shape, but the commit is irreversible, so the primary
  slot uses the destructive variant ("Delete this vault?" → *Cancel* / *Delete*). A
  destructive action always keeps a Cancel.

## Button width & layout

Button width is **responsive, not a single rule** — the two platforms differ on purpose, the
same way Apple's own alerts do (iOS fills the width; macOS right-aligns).

- **Desktop** — buttons are **right-aligned at their natural width**, not stretched. There's
  no thumb constraint with a mouse, and auto width keeps the primary emphasized while Cancel
  stays quiet; label length signals weight. Primary/destructive is rightmost.
- **Mobile** — buttons **fill the sheet width**. Two short buttons sit **side by side, 50/50**
  (Cancel left, primary right — see the bottom sheet below); **three or more, or long labels,
  stack** full-width instead. Full width is for thumb reach and touch targets.

Do **not** stretch buttons full-width on a desktop dialog: on a ~450px panel they look clumsy,
flatten the Cancel-vs-primary hierarchy, and waste the right-to-left scanning economy. (Carbon
is the deliberate exception — a whole-system edge-to-edge aesthetic, not a one-off.)

## Content & layout

A dialog is a **decision or a short form**, not a document. Keep it lean:

- **Minimal copy.** One line of context under the title, at most. Long explanations
  (how encryption works, where avatars come from) belong in docs or a help link, not stacked as
  paragraphs inside the dialog.
- **Short labels; never put essential info in a placeholder.** A placeholder is an example
  ("e.g. Triage Bot"), not instructions — it vanishes on type and fails contrast. If a field
  needs explanation, use a short label or a one-line hint under it.
- **One column, consistent fields.** Every field spans the width and uses the same
  [text field](/components/input) / [select](/components/select) treatment. Don't float a
  [segmented control](/components/segmented) off to the side — align it with the rest.
- **If it scrolls, it's a page.** A form with many fields (create-an-agent, full settings) is a
  route, not a modal. If a dialog must scroll, pin the title and the action footer so they stay
  visible.

## Tokens

Panel background from [`popover`](/foundations/color), radius
[`lg` (8px)](/foundations/radius), [`high`](/foundations/elevation) shadow, overlay at 50%
black.

## On mobile — bottom sheet

The same decision doesn't use a centered modal on mobile. A centered dialog fights the
thumb and the on-screen keyboard, so the mobile surface is a **bottom sheet**: it rises from
the bottom edge, its two actions sit side by side filling the width within thumb reach, and a
drag handle signals it's dismissible. Same content and semantics, platform-appropriate
presentation.

<BottomSheetShowcase />

This is the general rule for platform variants: keep the component's *meaning* identical
across surfaces, adapt its *presentation* to the platform. A [Select](/components/select)
follows the same web-dropdown → mobile-sheet pattern.

## Guidance

- One decision per dialog. If it grows into a multi-step flow or a long form, it's a page.
- Always offer a non-destructive way out (Cancel, click-outside, Escape / drag-down).
- Name the action on the button — "Delete", not "OK".
- Desktop: right-aligned auto-width actions. Mobile: full-width — 50/50 side by side for two,
  stacked for three or more.
