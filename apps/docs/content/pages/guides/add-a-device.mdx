---
title: Add a device
description: Pair a new device to your vault without typing your recovery phrase — using a 6-digit code and a safety-number check.
---

Adding a device is a two-sided handshake: the **new device** shows a short code, and an
**already-trusted device** approves it. You never type your recovery phrase. Under the hood this adds
the new device to your [signed roster](/how-it-works/devices-and-trust) and re-wraps your vault key to
it — instant, with no re-encryption of your notes.

You need one device that is already signed in to approve from.

## From the CLI

**On the new device**, start pairing:

```bash
notekit vault pair
```

It prints a **6-digit code** and a **safety number**, then waits.

**On a device that's already trusted**, approve it with that code:

```bash
notekit devices approve 123456
```

The approving device shows the same safety number and asks you to confirm. **Check that the two safety
numbers match** before you say yes — that's what rules out a machine-in-the-middle. Add `--yes` to skip
the prompt only if you've already compared them.

Once approved, the new device finishes automatically and can read your vault.

## From the app

1. On the new device, choose **Link a device** — it shows a 6-digit code and a safety number.
2. On a trusted device, open **Account → Devices → Link a device** and enter the code.
3. Compare the safety numbers on both screens, then **Approve**.

## What the safety number is for

The safety number is a short fingerprint of the new device's public key, shown on both screens. If they
match, no one intercepted the pairing. It's the same idea as verifying a contact in a messaging app —
see [devices & trust](/how-it-works/devices-and-trust#safety-numbers-mitm-detection).

<Callout type="warn">
If you lose every device, a new device can't be approved by another device — recover with your 24-word
phrase instead. Keep a second device paired so you always have an approver.
</Callout>
