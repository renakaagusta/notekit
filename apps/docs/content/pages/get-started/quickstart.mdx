title: Quickstart
description: Create an account, unlock your encrypted vault, and write your first note — in about five minutes.
---

## 1. Sign in

Open [app.notekit.online](https://app.notekit.online) and sign in. NoteKit uses your Google
account for identity — this authenticates you to the service; it does **not** give the service
access to your content, which is encrypted separately (see [Encryption](/how-it-works/encryption)).

## 2. Unlock your vault

The first time you sign in, NoteKit generates your encryption identity and a **24-word recovery
phrase**. This phrase is the master key to your vault.

- Write it down and keep it somewhere safe. NoteKit cannot recover it for you — that is the point
  of end-to-end encryption.
- Each additional device you add (desktop, mobile, CLI) is paired to your account without
  re-typing the phrase, using device-to-device linking.

## 3. Write a note

Create a note from the sidebar. As you type, the content is encrypted on your device and
committed to your Git vault. Everything is Markdown underneath.

## 4. Add another surface

- **Desktop** — download the Electron app and sign in with the same account.
- **Mobile** — install the iOS/Android app.
- **Terminal & agents** — see [Use the CLI](/guides/use-the-cli) and [Connect an agent](/guides/connect-an-agent).

## What just happened?

In those four steps you:

- created an identity whose keys live **only on your device** — the server never saw your recovery
  phrase or any private key;
- wrote a note that was **encrypted on your device** and committed to your Git vault as ciphertext;
- set up recovery (the phrase) and, optionally, more devices — each paired without re-typing the phrase.

That's the whole model: your data is encrypted before it leaves you, and the server just stores and
syncs ciphertext. The [How it works](/how-it-works) section explains why that holds.
